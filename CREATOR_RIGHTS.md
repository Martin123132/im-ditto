# Your I’m and your rights

You can adapt the designated Ditto starter to make an I’m for yourself, a business or a client. You can give your app away, sell it directly or choose another store. Keep the starter's MIT notice with the parts you copied, and respect other people's code, media and licences.

You choose the terms for your own contributions. Ditto does not take ownership of your app, private content or data. It also cannot guarantee ownership of AI-generated output. The starter notice does not automatically license your whole finished application as MIT.

The Core licence permits normal task-specific apps, hosting those apps and internal business use, without a company-size cutoff. It restricts taking protected host code and providing it as a general-purpose workspace platform or core-derived marketplace for others. It does not prohibit independently written compatible software. A video, game, accessibility or business app is not prohibited simply because we make something similar.

There is no compulsory I’m-Store listing or royalty on sales outside it. A future store would need its own seller agreement; it is not a live service or a condition of using the starter.

## What ships with your app

New dashboard drafts include LICENSE-DITTO-STARTER.txt and NOTICE-DITTO-STARTER.md. The normal package command includes them, and install/update keeps them inside the reviewed package. Keep them when adapting or distributing the starter. Add your own application terms separately rather than overwriting the starter notice.

Old generated apps and immutable preview packages are not silently edited. If updating an older starter-derived app, retain its existing terms and add appropriate notices for any newly copied code.

## Separate runtime terms

These creator permissions do not put the entire Ditto distribution under MIT. Read the [file schedule](LICENSE) and [Core licence](LICENSES/Im-Ditto-Core-1.0.md). The company launcher is included in the Core terms. Inherited Bridge files retain their legacy terms with the additional company runtime permission; third-party works retain their own licences. Earlier grants remain unchanged.
