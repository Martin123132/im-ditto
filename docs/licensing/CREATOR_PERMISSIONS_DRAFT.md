# I’m-Ditto creator permissions draft

Historical policy draft, 7 October 2026. Superseded for the unreleased source by [your creator rights](../../CREATOR_RIGHTS.md) and the terms identified in [LICENSE](../../LICENSE). This draft is not an additional grant. No solicitor review is claimed. The text below preserves the original policy discussion rather than defining today's terms.

## Make an I’m for your own work

The proposed policy would let individuals and organisations use Ditto to create and run their own I’ms for personal or commercial work. Normal creation would not require a commercial negotiation with us, even if your business grows.

## Give it away or sell it

You would be able to share an I’m, sell it directly, build it for a client or distribute it through a store of your choice. Joining I’m-Store would be optional. Any I’m-Store commission would come from a separate agreement covering transactions through that store, not every sale you make elsewhere.

## Keep control of your work

Using Ditto would not assign your original contributions, private content or data to Two Hands Network Ltd. You would choose terms for the rights you hold in your app. You would still need permission for third-party material you include; neither Ditto nor this policy can guarantee ownership of every AI-generated output.

The designated starter would come with a permissive licence. You would keep its notices with copied code, while licensing your own additions separately. Its permissions would not automatically cover the Ditto host, example media or other dependencies.

## Build in the same fields as us

Your app would not become prohibited just because we also offer an app for video, music, education, games, accessibility or the same business problem. The legal terms should expressly protect that freedom, including when the company introduces a similar product later.

## Respect the separate platform boundary

This creator permission is intended for apps built with Ditto. It is not intended to permit taking the restricted host code, changing its name and offering it as a competing general-purpose Ditto platform or core-derived store. The final terms must define that boundary clearly; an app’s label alone should not decide it.

The permissive starter remains permissive. The policy must not claim to prohibit an independently developed compatible implementation merely because it competes. Restrictions can only reach material and conduct actually covered by valid rights and terms.

## Describe compatibility honestly

Creators should be able to say their app works with I’m-Ditto without implying that it is made, audited or endorsed by us. Brand and logo permissions would be separate. Package integrity checks do not certify an app’s safety.

## Keep purchasing and trust separate

A software licence does not grant an app access to a user’s computer. Installation and access remain owner-controlled. Payment, a store listing or a familiar name must not silently widen those permissions.

For the proposed file boundaries and legal decisions, see the [licensing proposal](LICENSING_PROPOSAL.md).
